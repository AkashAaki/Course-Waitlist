# AI Usage Record

AI tool: Claude Code (Claude Opus 5.5), used throughout as an engineering assistant.

**Operating principle:** agents execute under defined autonomy boundaries; the engineer owns oversight, approvals and final
quality. The boundaries came from the [system prompt](prompts/system-prompt.md): phase gates, a hard scope list, an invariant
checklist, and a rule that the AI may not claim a result the engineer has not seen. Within them, the AI proposed, implemented
and ran checks. Every policy and design decision was the engineer's.

**Evidence is labelled by who produced it.** *AI-run* means the AI executed it in its own session. *Engineer-run* means
the engineer executed it on their own machine; that output is kept in [evidence/engineer-runs.md](evidence/engineer-runs.md), and nothing in that column comes from
anywhere else. Only fake learner IDs (L1, L2, …) are used. No credentials, secrets or personal data appear in prompts.

### Prompt iteration and lessons
_Drafted by the AI from the record above. For the engineer to edit and confirm._

1. **An ambiguous instruction hands the decision to the AI.** "update to trim typecase and other formats" (**4**) was
   clear on trimming, probably meant case, and left "other formats" open. The AI had to interpret it. It chose NFKC, trim
   and upper-case. It deliberately excluded separators, internal spaces and leading zeros, and flagged that choice for
   confirmation. The result was sound, but the scope was set by the AI's interpretation, not by me. How I would phrase it now:
   > Normalise learner and course IDs before every comparison: NFKC, then trim, then upper-case. These must equal `L1`:
   > `l1`, `' l1 '`, `'Ｌ１'`. These must stay distinct from `L1`: `L-1`, `L 1`, `L01`. Pin both lists in tests.
2. **"Start next phase" is an implicit approval.** In **5** the AI had to infer three decisions from one short phrase:
   sign-off on DESIGN.md §4–§6, acceptance of choices 1, 2 and 4 from entry 3, and separators not being folded. A gate
   should record what is approved, for example: "Approved: DESIGN §4–§6. Entry 3 choices 1, 2 and 4 accepted; choice 3
   replaced by entry 4. Start Phase 2." Implicit approvals are also why every Status still needs marking now.
3. **Ask for the AI's interpretation, then test it against the model.** One question (**0a**) surfaced the real design
   question. But the AI's first answer, that eligibility can change, didn't fit a service that never records completions,
   and it took three challenges (**0c, 0f, 0k**) to settle. A confident AI interpretation is a hypothesis to test, not a
   requirement.
4. **A verification rule only produces evidence if the human side of the loop closes.** "Don't claim tests pass until
   I've shared output" made the AI label its evidence carefully. But during the session I pasted output back only for
   Phase 0. The rest was recovered afterwards from terminal history, cut off by `tail`, and with no run of entry 7's
   state. Next time: paste the full output at each phase gate, in the same message as the go-ahead.

These lessons are applied in a revised system prompt and two prompt templates, written after the assessment and labelled
as such: [system-prompt.md § v2](prompts/system-prompt.md#revised-version-v2-post-assessment) and
[key prompts § 7](prompts/key-prompts.md#7-reusable-prompt-templates-post-assessment).
